## Physical delivery success on repeatedly trained tasks

| Existing backprop-free Brain arm | Before learning | After 4,000 physical decisions | 95% bootstrap CI on after success | Nearby diagnostic variants |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Native three-action pickup/move/drop | 2.50% | 10.00% | [4.17%, 18.33%] | 5.00% |
| Native **two-action** pickup/drop (different task) | 10.00% | 97.50% | [95.00%, 100.00%] | 4.58% |
| Goal masked, three-action | 2.50% | 9.17% | [4.17%, 14.17%] | 4.17% |

| No learning reward, three-action | 2.50% | 2.50% | [0.00%, 5.00%] | 2.08% |

On the native three-action task, mean paired seed improvement from before training was +7.50 percentage points, exploratory 95% paired CI [+1.67, +15.83] pp. This is a positive fixed-task observation but **not evidence of transfer**. The prior fixed-task study used different frozen evaluation streams and obtained 9.17% after 4,000 decisions; both estimates describe weak three-action acquisition, not a validated reproducible skill across different tasks.

## Adversarial findings and necessary qualifications

1. **Direct sensory access exists:** In the inspected [Brain.encode code](../game/world/brain.js), the complete public observation is copied into the direct actor and critic inputs. The frozen trained native policy's mean L1 change when the visible target was swapped is only **0.0844**; the goal-masked arm shows exactly **0** and passed leakage tests. Neither nonzero policy sensitivity nor an L1 norm alone proves correct conditional goal following.
2. **Two-action success is not a causal isolation of delay:** The two-action pickup/drop task changes the goal position, physical state and available reward sequence, as well as minimal depth. The 97.5%-versus-10% difference establishes an engineered task-difficulty gap, **not** an isolated causal effect of temporal credit assignment.
3. **Eligibility and TD bootstrap interventions reduce task success:** The learner with previous-step traces cleared scored 0%, and the learner without next-value bootstrap scored 2.5%, against 10% native. Each intervention changes only the corresponding local update rule while preserving physical budget and initial architecture, but resulting training trajectories diverge; no identical-transition counterfactual update test or sealed multi-family replication has been completed.
4. **Masking target on a constant-goal training task is weak:** 9.17% success without goal input is possible through memorization of one fixed action pattern; this does not demonstrate goal-independent reasoning or that Brain follows visible target information.
5. **No-feedback control:** With zero learner reward and a zero-initialized critic, mean absolute TD error remained 0; final physical success was 2.5%, consistent with chance actions. This is a sanity check, not a biological plasticity finding.
6. **Limitations:** Ten seeds with bootstrap intervals are exploratory, multiple comparisons were not corrected, scoring repeats reuse task structures, the model is a toy 5×5 board, and target variants are unsealed exploratory probes. Equal physical-decision budgets are not equal experienced trajectories across ablations.
