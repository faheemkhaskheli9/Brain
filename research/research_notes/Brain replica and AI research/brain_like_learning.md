# How the brain learns vs today's AI, and brain-inspired learning algorithms (state as of 2026-10-12)

Labels used below: **[ESTABLISHED]** = textbook/peer-reviewed and replicated; **[EVIDENCE]** = a single peer-reviewed experimental result, not yet replicated; **[CLAIM]** = author/company claim, not independently verified; **[HYPOTHESIS]** = proposal or open question. Citations marked *(recall)* are classic papers cited from prior knowledge by DOI and were not re-fetched in this session; all other sources were opened or searched on 2026-10-12.

Compared with the owner's KB (`learning/brain-inspired-learning-algorithms.md`, `guides/build-a-brain-like-learner.md`, last verified 2026-10-11): the KB covers Oja/Hebb, predictive coding, three-factor actor-critic, STDP, EWC, replay and curiosity. It does **not** record: the Feb 2026 Nature dendritic vectorized-error result, burst-dependent plasticity, feedback alignment / equilibrium propagation / forward-forward, BabyLM, HRM/TRM, BDH, SpikingBrain, Monty, AXIOM, LFM2, the loss-of-plasticity result, or the 2026 forward-forward scaling limits. All of those are flagged **NEW** below.

## (a) Is it known which learning rule the cortex uses?

### Takeaway
No. As of October 2026 no single cortical learning rule is established. Local Hebbian/STDP plasticity and dopamine-gated three-factor plasticity are established as mechanisms; whether and how cortex solves deep credit assignment is open. The strongest new evidence (Francioni et al., Nature, Feb 2026) shows **neuron-specific (vectorized), sign-correct error-like signals in apical dendrites that are needed for learning**, which favours backprop-like or target-propagation-like schemes over a single broadcast reward signal, but does not pick one algorithm.

### Cited Findings
- **[ESTABLISHED] framing review:** Lillicrap, Santoro, Marris, Akerman & Hinton (2020) "Backpropagation and the brain", *Nat Rev Neurosci* 21:335-346, argue the brain could approximate backprop by using differences in neural activity (feedforward vs feedback-driven) to encode errors ("NGRAD" family), while exact backprop (symmetric weights, separate error phase) is implausible — [doi:10.1038/s41583-020-0277-3](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41583-020-0277-3) *(recall)*.
- **[ESTABLISHED] three-factor rules:** Hebbian eligibility traces gated by a third neuromodulatory factor (dopamine as reward prediction error) — Frémaux & Gerstner 2016 review, [doi:10.3389/fncir.2015.00085](https://doi.org/10.3389/fncir.2015.00085) *(recall)*; dopamine RPE: Schultz, Dayan & Montague 1997, [doi:10.1126/science.275.5306.1593](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.275.5306.1593) (already in KB). A scalar global signal scales poorly to large networks; this is the problem the vectorized-signal work addresses [HYPOTHESIS about scaling, widely argued in Lillicrap 2020].
- **[HYPOTHESIS, model] feedback alignment:** random fixed feedback weights can train deep nets, removing the weight-symmetry requirement — Lillicrap et al. 2016, *Nat Commun* 7:13276, [doi:10.1038/ncomms13276](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms13276) *(recall)*. Lags backprop on large vision tasks; a 2026 preprint says a local Forward-Forward variant reaches "more than three times the accuracy of feedback alignment on AlexNet" at ImageNet-1K — [arXiv:2605.00082](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.00082) (snippet only).
- **[HYPOTHESIS, model] burst-dependent plasticity:** Payeur, Guerguiev, Zenke, Richards & Naud (2021), *Nat Neurosci* 24:1010-1019 — bursts vs single spikes multiplex feedforward and feedback (error) signals in pyramidal neurons; the rule approximates backprop in hierarchical nets — [doi:10.1038/s41593-021-00857-x](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-021-00857-x) *(recall)*.
- **[HYPOTHESIS, model] equilibrium propagation:** Scellier & Bengio 2017, energy-based nets with a free and a weakly-clamped (nudged) phase; local contrastive updates equal the gradient in the small-nudge limit — [doi:10.3389/fncom.2017.00024](https://doi.org/10.3389/fncom.2017.00024) *(recall)*. I found **no ImageNet-1K-scale EqProp result** in this search.
- **[HYPOTHESIS, model] forward-forward:** Hinton 2022, two forward passes (positive/negative data) with layer-local "goodness" objective — [arXiv:2212.13345](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.13345) *(recall)*.
- **[HYPOTHESIS, model] predictive coding approximates backprop** under small-error conditions — Whittington & Bogacz 2017, [doi:10.1162/NECO_a_00949](https://doi.org/10.1162/NECO_a_00949) (already in KB).
- **NEW [EVIDENCE] Feb 2026, Nature:** Francioni, Tang, Toloza, Ding, Brown & Harnett, "Vectorized instructive signals in cortical dendrites", *Nature* 652:1254-1263 (published 25 Feb 2026; bioRxiv 13 Jan 2025). Mice learned a BCI task by modulating two intermingled groups of L5 pyramidal neurons in retrosplenial cortex. Distal apical dendrites carried reward/error information whose **sign depended on each neuron's causal role** and predicted each neuron's change over learning; a scalar (same-signal-for-all) hypothesis did not fit. Optogenetic activation of NDNF+ layer-1 interneurons abolished the vectorized dendritic signals **and disrupted learning**. Authors call it "the first biological evidence of a vectorized solution to the credit assignment problem in the brain"; signals look like "error derivative rather than error itself", consistent with difference target propagation as well as backprop-like models — [PMC13112360](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13112360/), [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10190-7).
- **NEW [EVIDENCE] 2026, Nat Neurosci:** learning errors during rule switching were associated with reduced activity of layer-1 NDNF interneurons, which gate dendritic calcium signals — ["Flexibility begins in the dendrites"](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41593-026-02336-7) (search snippet only).
- **NEW [HYPOTHESIS, model] 2025-2026:** "'Backpropagation and the brain' realized in cortical error neuron microcircuits" proposes separate error (L2/3) and representation (L5) pyramidal populations whose phase-free dynamics approximate backprop; later in PLOS Comp Biol 2026 — [bioRxiv 2025.07.11.664263](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.07.11.664263v1.full), [PMC13089762](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13089762/). "Backpropagation through space, time and the brain" (Nat Commun, Dec 2025) uses dendritic structure + prospective coding for an online approximation of backprop-through-time — [s41467-025-66666-z](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-66666-z).
- **NEW [state of the field] local rules at scale:** a 2026 preprint states that, as of its cutoff, no local rule had shown backprop parity on full ImageNet-1K — [arXiv:2608.19995](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.19995) (snippet). Hyperspherical Forward-Forward reports **25.70% top-1 on ImageNet-1K**, ~2 points below SoftHebb — [arXiv:2605.00082](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.00082) (snippet). Chen (Jun 2026, rev. Oct 2026) "Synthetic Benchmarks Overstate Forward-Forward Scaling": best FF variant DTG-FF reaches **49.4% on ImageNet-100 vs 75.6%** for a BP-trained ResNet-50 (SSL + linear eval); BP-DeepSup beats it by 2.40 pp (CIFAR-10) and 5.93 pp (CIFAR-100), the gap growing with class count; FF also used **more** memory (7.90 GB vs 4.18 GB) and was slower (138 vs 157 img/s) than BP with gradient accumulation — [arXiv:2606.06539](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06539). Self-Contrastive Forward-Forward (Nat Commun 2025): 35.67% top-1 on Tiny ImageNet — [s41467-025-61037-0](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-61037-0). Claims of beating BP (Synergistic Information Distillation: +6.8% Tiny-ImageNet, +4.3% CIFAR-100) are unverified preprints — [arXiv:2510.03273](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.03273).

### Inferences
- The 2026 Harnett result moves "cortex uses per-neuron error signals in apical dendrites" from hypothesis toward evidence, which weakens pure-three-factor (scalar dopamine only) accounts **for cortex** but leaves the exact rule (backprop approximation, target prop, burst-dependent) open. One study, one area (RSC), one artificial BCI task: replication in sensory/motor cortex with natural tasks is the obvious next step.
- Engineering-wise, local rules still trail backprop badly at ImageNet scale (roughly 25% vs ~75%+ top-1), and the "memory-saving" argument for FF did not survive a fair baseline in 2026. Biological plausibility and engineering competitiveness remain separate goals.

### Gaps
- No full text read for the 2026 Nat Neurosci NDNF paper or arXiv:2608.19995 / 2605.00082 (snippets only); numbers from those should be checked.
- No 2024-2026 large-scale EqProp result found. No direct experimental test of burst-dependent plasticity as a credit-assignment mechanism found in this pass.

## (b) Sample efficiency: humans vs AI

### Takeaway
Children reach fluent language on roughly 10^7-10^8 words; frontier LLMs use ~10^13 tokens, a gap of about 3-6 orders of magnitude. BabyLM (2023-2025) shows models trained on 10-100M words can be surprisingly good on benchmarks, but best results come from new objectives/architectures, not curricula, and human-scale models are still not child-like learners. Deep nets also suffer catastrophic forgetting and, more fundamentally, loss of plasticity under continual training, which brains do not show in the same way.

### Cited Findings
- **Child input:** a preteen in a linguistically rich home hears ~**100M words**; ~**300M by age 20** with literacy; toddlers produce grammatical sentences after ~**10M** (up to 30M) words — [MIT Technology Review, 24 Aug 2026](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/08/24/1141740/kids-machines-language-learning/).
- **LLM input:** Llama 3.1 pretrained on **15 trillion tokens**; frontier models possibly ~10x more (Wilcox) — same [MIT TR article](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/08/24/1141740/kids-machines-language-learning/). Frank (2023, *Trends Cogn Sci*): LLMs need "billions of words" for zero-shot/in-context behaviour that children show with "a few million"; gap of **3-4, sometimes up to 6, orders of magnitude** — [ANL talk abstract](https://www.anl.gov/event/bridging-the-data-gap-between-children-and-large-language-models), [CCN 2024 keynote](https://2024.ccneuro.org/keynote-michael-frank/); a child would need ~**100K years of speech** to match top LLM text volume (Lake citing Frank 2023) — [NYU CIMS](https://cims.nyu.edu/ai/events/714/). Paper PDF: [Frank 2023](https://sites.socsci.uci.edu/~lpearl/colareadinggroup/readings/Frank2023_ChildrenLLMs.pdf). Candidate explanations [HYPOTHESIS]: prior conceptual knowledge, multimodal grounding, social/interactive input.
- **Quotes** (MIT TR 2026): Frank: "If you train GPT-2 on 30 million words, you get a nonsense generator; you don't get a kid." Warstadt: "There was never a time when people were training language models at human scale where we were impressed by them." Gopnik: children are "actively choosing their own data" — [MIT TR](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/08/24/1141740/kids-machines-language-learning/).
- **BabyLM budgets & results:** 100M-word "strict" and 10M-word "strict-small" tracks. Curriculum learning was the most popular approach in round 1 but underperformed expectations; 2024 winner **GPT-BERT** (~100M words) beat Llama 2 70B (trained on ~15,000x more) on one benchmark; in 2024, learning by interacting with other models did not beat standard training — [MIT TR](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/08/24/1141740/kids-machines-language-learning/).
- **BabyLM 2025 (3rd challenge, Nov 2025):** added an **interaction track** (students learn from teacher-model feedback), "human-likeness" evals, a training-compute cap and checkpoint evaluation. Findings: new training objectives and architectures gave the best results; teacher interaction can give high-quality models; strict-small and interaction submissions beat baselines; "We do not observe a complete correlation between training FLOPs and performance" — Charpentier et al. 2025, [ACL Anthology 2025.babylm-main.28](https://aclanthology.org/2025.babylm-main.28). Note: the April 2025 arXiv "Findings of the BabyLM Challenge" ([2504.08165](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.08165v1)) covers an earlier round.
- **Grounded one-child learning:** a 2024 model trained on **61 hours** of one child's SAYCam headcam footage learned simple word-referent mappings ("ball", "cat") — [MIT TR](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/08/24/1141740/kids-machines-language-learning/); Vong, Wang, Orhan & Lake 2024, *Science* 383:504 — [doi:10.1126/science.adi1374](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adi1374) *(recall)*.
- **Continual learning:** **[ESTABLISHED]** catastrophic forgetting; EWC (Kirkpatrick 2017, [doi:10.1073/pnas.1611835114](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1611835114), in KB). **NEW to KB [EVIDENCE]:** Dohare et al. 2024 *Nature* "Loss of plasticity in deep continual learning": standard deep learning gradually loses plasticity until it "learns no better than a shallow network" (ImageNet and RL); fix = **continual backpropagation**, which reinitializes a small fraction of low-utility units; authors argue sustained learning needs "a random, non-gradient component" — [doi:10.1038/s41586-024-07711-7](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07711-7), [Amii](https://www.amii.ca/research/loss-of-plasticity-in-deep-continual-learning). Up to ~90% of units may become dead in long runs (Mahmood, press) — [TechXplore](https://techxplore.com/news/2024-08-team-solution-ai-problem.html).
- **One-shot learning in the brain [ESTABLISHED]:** hippocampal behavioural-timescale synaptic plasticity forms a place field in a single trial (Bittner et al. 2017, *Science* 357:1033) — [doi:10.1126/science.aan3846](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aan3846) *(recall)*. Meta-learning can give networks human-like systematic generalization from few examples (Lake & Baroni 2023, *Nature* 623:115) — [doi:10.1038/s41586-023-06668-3](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06668-3) *(recall)*.

### Inferences
- The sample-efficiency gap is real but partly confounded: children get multimodal, embodied, interactive, self-selected data plus evolved priors; LLMs get passive text. BabyLM's "no full FLOPs-performance correlation" and the poor showing of curricula suggest the gap is not just about data order.
- Neural nets have two distinct continual-learning failures (forgetting old tasks, and losing the ability to learn new ones). The second is less known and maps naturally onto brain mechanisms (neurogenesis, synaptic turnover) [HYPOTHESIS].

### Gaps
- No precise peer-reviewed figure for words heard **by age 10** specifically; ~100M "preteen" is a journalistic estimate (MIT TR). Published estimates vary widely with socioeconomic setting (not fetched here).
- BabyLM 2025 winner names/scores not extracted (abstract only).

## (c) Brain-inspired approaches in 2025-2026: status and what one person can build

### Takeaway
2025-2026 brought several high-profile "brain-inspired" models, but most borrow brain **principles** (recurrence/iteration, sparsity, Hebbian fast weights, spiking-style activations, sensorimotor columns) on top of backprop training. Independent checks often shrink claims (HRM's hierarchy mattered little; its refinement loop did). For one person: TRM/HRM-style recursive tiny models, Monty, BDH at small scale, and local-rule (FF/predictive coding/three-factor) experiments on CIFAR/MNIST are all realistic on one GPU; SpikingBrain-scale or LFM-scale models are not.

### Cited Findings
- **NEW HRM (Jun 2025):** Hierarchical Reasoning Model, 27M parameters, two recurrent modules at different timescales (brain-inspired), trained on ~1,000 examples — [arXiv:2506.21734](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.21734). **Claimed** 41% ARC-AGI-1 public eval; **ARC Prize verified 32%** on ARC-AGI-1 semi-private and **2%** on ARC-AGI-2. Ablations: a same-size transformer came within ~5 pp without tuning; the **outer refinement loop** was the main driver (1→2 loops +~13 pp; 1→8 loops ~doubled score); performance came largely from task augmentation and memorizing evaluation-task solutions, with limited cross-task transfer — [ARC Prize analysis, 15 Aug 2025](https://arcprize.org/blog/hrm-analysis).
- **NEW TRM (Oct 2025):** Tiny Recursive Model, **7M parameters, 2 layers**, simpler successor to HRM: **44.6% ARC-AGI-1, 7.8% ARC-AGI-2** vs HRM 40.3%/5.0% in the same paper's table — [arXiv:2510.04871](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.04871). Uses ~1000x augmentation per example, so LLM comparisons are not like-for-like ([HF discussion via search](https://huggingface-gw-kz.just-ai.com/papers/2510.04871)). Feb 2026 follow-up: Mamba-2 hybrid 45.88% vs 43.88% pass@2 — [arXiv:2602.12078](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.12078v2). Context: ARC Prize 2025 top verified commercial model 37.6% on ARC-AGI-2, a Gemini 3 Pro refinement solution 54% — [ARC Prize 2025 results](https://arcprize.org/blog/arc-prize-2025-results-analysis).
- **NEW BDH "Dragon Hatchling" (Pathway, 30 Sep 2025):** Kosowski, Uznański, Chorowski, Stamirowska, Bartoszkiewicz. Scale-free graph of locally interacting "neuron particles"; inference-time working memory via **Hebbian synaptic plasticity** with spiking-like neurons; sparse, positive, reportedly monosemantic activations; **[CLAIM]** GPT-2-comparable on language/translation at 10M-1B parameters with the same data — [arXiv:2509.26507](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.26507), code [github.com/pathwaycom/bdh](https://github.com/pathwaycom/bdh). Note: weights are still trained by backprop; Hebbian dynamics act as fast state. Company-side claims (29.5% pass@2 ARC-AGI-1 for "BDH-CQ", reproductions by NYU/Bielik AI, NVIDIA/AWS deployment) are unverified secondary reports — [blog review](https://blog.gopenai.com/paper-review-the-dragon-hatchling-the-missing-link-between-the-transformer-and-models-of-the-48f31eae7513).
- **NEW SpikingBrain (CAS Institute of Automation, Sep 2025):** SpikingBrain-7B (linear) and SpikingBrain-76B (hybrid-linear MoE), adaptive spiking neurons + linear/sliding-window attention; converted from pretrained Transformers with ~**150B tokens** (<2% of typical data); trained on MetaX (non-NVIDIA) GPUs; **[CLAIM]** >100x faster time-to-first-token at 4M-token context for 7B, ~69% spiking sparsity — [arXiv:2509.05276](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.05276v1). Spikes are simulated on GPUs; energy gains require neuromorphic hardware — [commentary](https://arxiviq.substack.com/p/spikingbrain-technical-report-spiking).
- **NEW Thousand Brains Project / Monty:** Leadholm, Clay, Knudstrup, Lee & Hawkins (Jul 2025) "Thousand-Brains Systems": cortical-column-inspired sensorimotor learning on YCB 3D objects; robust generalization, captures object symmetries, voting across columns speeds inference, **Hebbian-like binding gives fast continual learning**; authors call Monty "nascent" — [arXiv:2507.04494](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.04494); framework paper Dec 2024 [arXiv:2412.18354](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.18354). Project became a non-profit in 2025; first real-world sensor movement; 2026 goals = core capabilities, platform quality, community, "Solutions Incubator" — [TBP forum, Jan 2026](https://thousandbrains.discourse.group/t/2026-01-2025-review-2026-direction-goals/936). No 2026 benchmark paper found.
- **Liquid / continuous-time nets:** liquid time-constant networks (Hasani et al., AAAI 2021) — [arXiv:2006.04439](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.04439) *(recall)*. Liquid AI's **LFM2** (tech report [arXiv:2511.23404](https://huggingface.co/LiquidAI/papers), Nov 2025) uses gated short convolutions + grouped-query attention; **[CLAIM]** up to 2x faster CPU decode/prefill than Qwen3; LFM2.5 models released since — [Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/LiquidAI/LFM2.5-2.6B-Base). Link from LTC theory to LFM2's design is only claimed by third parties — unverified.
- **Active inference — VERSES AXIOM (Jun 2025):** object-centric active-inference agent; **[CLAIM]** "up to 60% better, 97% more efficient and learns 39 times faster" than DreamerV3 on Gameworld 10K (10 arcade games); validation was by a firm VERSES commissioned, not peer review — [VERSES press release](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2025/06/02/3091981/0/en/VERSES-Digital-Brain-Beats-Google-s-Top-AI-At-Gameworld-10k-Atari-Challenge.html).
- **Local rules at scale:** see section (a) — best ImageNet-1K local-loss ~25.7% top-1 (2026), FF trails BP increasingly with class count (2026).
- **Biology vs principles — Zador:** Zador 2019 "A critique of pure learning", *Nat Commun* 10:3770: much animal behaviour is innate; the genome (~1 GB) cannot specify the synaptic wiring explicitly, so wiring rules pass through a **genomic bottleneck** that acts as a regularizer, and AI should seek the right inductive biases rather than copy all learning — [doi:10.1038/s41467-019-11786-6](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-019-11786-6) *(recall)*; same argument in Zador's NeurIPS 2020 talk — [NeurIPS](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2020/protected/invited_16167.html). Model: a small "g-network" compresses a larger "p-network" and still yields innate ability — [bioRxiv 2021.03.16.435261](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/2021.03.16.435261.full.pdf) (published version reportedly Shuvaev, Lachi, Koulakov & Zador, PNAS 2024 — not confirmed in this session). A 2024 NeuroAI review describes the genome as lossy compression of ancestral experience — [arXiv:2411.18526](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.18526). Zador et al. 2023 NeuroAI (in KB) argues for principles via an "embodied Turing test". Frank 2023 likewise stresses grounding/social input rather than biophysical detail.

### Inferences
- **Realistic for one person (1 consumer GPU), ranked:**
  1. TRM (7M params) reproduction and ablations on Sudoku/Maze/ARC — public code, small, tests "iterative refinement" which ARC Prize found to be the active ingredient.
  2. Local-learning benchmark: FF / predictive coding / three-factor / feedback alignment vs backprop on CIFAR-10/100, reporting the gap vs class count (replicates the 2026 FF-scaling finding cheaply).
  3. Continual backprop vs standard backprop on a permuted/streaming task (Dohare 2024) — small and directly about brain-like lifelong learning.
  4. BDH at 10M-100M params on a small corpus (public code) — tests Hebbian fast-weight memory.
  5. Monty on YCB in simulation (open-source) — sensorimotor, continual learning.
  6. A BabyLM strict-small (10M words) model — feasible on one GPU.
  - Not realistic: SpikingBrain-76B, LFM2-scale pretraining, ImageNet-1K local-rule training beyond small ResNets.
- **Principles vs exact biology:** the evidence leans toward "principles": the most successful 2025 "brain-inspired" systems are trained with backprop and borrow structure (recurrence, sparsity, fast Hebbian state, columns). Whether brain-level sample efficiency needs biologically exact local learning is **[HYPOTHESIS/open]**; the 2026 dendritic result suggests the brain itself does a vectorized, backprop-like credit assignment, which would make "exact local rule" less important than "good credit assignment + good priors + grounded interactive data".

### Gaps
- No independent replication found for BDH, AXIOM or SpikingBrain claims.
- No peer-reviewed 2026 Monty benchmark; HRM/TRM peer-review status not checked.
- No source found for any local learning rule reaching backprop-level accuracy on ImageNet-1K or LLM-scale language modelling as of Oct 2026.
