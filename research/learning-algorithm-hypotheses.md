# Hypotheses: the brain's learning algorithm

**Research thread** (owner, started 2026-10-11). This file is the place to resume the
thread from any machine or chat session: open it, read **Next up** and the **Log**, then
carry on.

## The problem
We know some local learning rules: Hebbian learning, STDP and dopamine as a
reward-prediction error. No known rule explains how humans learn abstraction, language or
reasoning. LLMs, Claude included, get those abilities from scale and backpropagation, not
by working like a brain (see
[build-a-brain-like-learner](../guides/build-a-brain-like-learner.md)).

The working question:

> Can rules that are **local** (each synapse sees only its own two neurons plus broadcast
> signals such as dopamine) learn **reusable abstract structure**? If not, what extra
> ingredient does the brain add?

## How to read this file
- **Every row is a hypothesis, not an established fact.** A source means "this idea was
  proposed or argued in that paper", not "that paper proved it".
- All 185 cited sources were checked on 2026-10-12 (title, first author and year
  against Crossref, Semantic Scholar or OpenAlex; 12 that the databases missed or mismatched
  were checked by hand against arXiv, PMLR, CVF and Crossref pages) and
  each citation links to its official DOI, proceedings or arXiv page. The full list, with
  open-access PDFs, is [learning-algorithm-sources.md](learning-algorithm-sources.md).
  Each paper's methods, experiments and key results, and whether it really supports its
  row, are in [learning-algorithm-notes/](learning-algorithm-notes/README.md).
  A verified citation shows the paper exists and says roughly this. Whether the
  hypothesis is still open in the literature is step 0 of
  [research-with-ai](../guides/research-with-ai.md), done per row.
- Rows marked **new** come from this research thread and have no published source.
- Many rows compete (for example H001 vs H090: "the brain approximates gradients" vs
  "there is no special algorithm, only scale"). A result that supports one weakens the
  other. Rows can also combine: the brain probably uses several of these at once.
- **Tier** says what can answer the question. T1 = a simulation in this repo (days to
  weeks). T2 = open data (weeks). T3 = needs a lab, new instruments or theory.
- **Status** is one of: `open`, `in progress`, `supported`, `weakened`, `refuted`,
  `answered elsewhere`. Change it when there is evidence, and add a Log line.

**Plain-language version.** Every row is explained in simple words, with a step-by-step
workflow of how it would work in the brain and how to test it, in
[hypotheses-explained/](hypotheses-explained/README.md).

## A. Credit assignment: local approximations of backprop
| ID | Hypothesis | Source | Test → counts against it if | Tier | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| H001 | Predictive coding networks compute weight updates that converge to backprop's gradients, using only local errors. | [Whittington & Bogacz 2017](https://doi.org/10.1162/neco_a_00949), *Neural Comput* | Compare PC updates with backprop gradients on a tiny MLP → cosine similarity stays low at equilibrium | T1 | supported (#92: cosine 1.000 with output variance 100x; 0.83-0.96 with equal variances) |
| H002 | Fixed random feedback weights are enough: the forward weights learn to align with them (feedback alignment). | [Lillicrap et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1038/ncomms13276), *Nat Commun* | FA vs backprop on MNIST/XOR → FA fails to learn hidden layers (the paper passes this: FA 2.1% vs backprop 2.4% test error on MNIST; run as a replication) | T1 | supported (#92: replicated on 8x8 digits, FA 4.5% vs BP 3.7% vs readout only 12.9%) |
| H003 | The output error can be broadcast straight to every layer through random weights (direct feedback alignment). | [Nøkland 2016](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.1609.01596), NeurIPS | DFA on a 3-layer MLP → no better than training only the readout (the paper passes this: DFA 1.74% vs backprop 1.60% on MNIST; run as a replication) | T1 | supported (#92: replicated on 8x8 digits, DFA 4.2% vs BP 3.7% vs readout only 12.9%) |
| H004 | Pyramidal neurons multiplex: event rate carries the feedforward signal and burst probability carries the error. | [Payeur et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-021-00857-x), *Nat Neurosci* | Burst-dependent rule in a spiking net → cannot solve XOR | T1/T3 | open |
| H005 | Apical dendrites carry an error: in Sacramento it is top-down input minus a learned lateral-interneuron prediction; in Urbanczik & Senn the basal rule compares somatic spiking with the dendritic prediction. | [Sacramento et al. 2018](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.1810.11393), NeurIPS; [Urbanczik & Senn 2014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2013.11.030), *Neuron* | Dendritic microcircuit model → updates do not track backprop | T1 | open |

| H010 | Perturbing activity or weights and correlating the change with reward is enough (node perturbation, REINFORCE). | H011 | Two forward passes, one on real data and one on negative data, with a local goodness per layer replace backward passes (forward-forward). | [Hinton 2022](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2212.13345), arXiv:2212.13345 | FF on MNIST → no better than a random-feature readout | T1 | open |
| H012 | Alternating a wake phase and a sleep phase trains recognition and generative paths with local rules (wake-sleep). | [Hinton et al. 1995](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.7761831), *Science* | Wake-sleep on toy data → generative model does not improve | T1 | open |
| H013 | The thalamus (pulvinar) supplies the "outcome" phase: every ~100 ms alpha cycle compares the cortical prediction with actual input. | [O'Reilly et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1162/jocn_a_01708), *J Cogn Neurosci* | T1 model works; T3: pulvinar silencing should block predictive learning (an adaptation; the paper's own predictions are blocking 5IB bursting and 6CT-to-pulvinar plasticity) | T1/T3 | open |
| H014 | Learning across time uses eligibility traces plus a broadcast learning signal, not backprop through time (e-prop). | [Bellec et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-020-17236-y), *Nat Commun* | e-prop vs BPTT on a delayed-recall task → e-prop fails at delays over a few seconds (untested: the paper shows delays up to about 1-2 s) | T1 | open |
| H015 | The cerebellum predicts the future error signal for cortex, so cortex can learn without waiting. | H016 | Each layer optimises its own local loss (which still uses the labels), so no global, backpropagated error is needed. | [Nøkland & Eidnes 2019](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.1901.06656), ICML | Local-loss net → large gap to end-to-end training on structured tasks (the paper passes this on CIFAR/STL; run as a replication) | T1 | open |
| H017 | Attention feedback tags the neurons that caused an action, and a global reward signal then updates only the tagged synapses (AGREL; the dopamine assignment is from the 2018 review). | [Roelfsema & van Ooyen 2005](https://doi.org/10.1162/0899766054615699), *Neural Comput*; [Roelfsema & Holtmaat 2018](https://doi.org/10.1038/nrn.2018.6), *Nat Rev Neurosci* | AGREL on a classification task → much slower than backprop | T1 | open |
| H018 | Credit assignment is learned: the feedback pathway is itself trained (learning to learn) together with local plasticity. | [Lindsey & Litwin-Kumar 2020](https://doi.org/10.48550/arxiv.2006.09549), NeurIPS | Meta-learned feedback → no gain over fixed random feedback (not tested in the paper; its meta-learning is an offline outer loop) | T1 | open |
| H019 | Somatic spikes are predicted by dendrites, and the prediction error at the dendrite drives plasticity. | [Urbanczik & Senn 2014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuron.2013.11.030), *Neuron* | One-neuron model → cannot learn a teacher's input-output map | T1 | open |
| H020 | Backprop-like learning happens only in slow, sleep-linked phases, not online. | **new** (nearest: Hinton 1995 wake-sleep; Hinton 2022 section 5 on separating phases. Neither claims this.) | Online-only vs sleep-phase learning of the same rule → no difference | T1 | open |
