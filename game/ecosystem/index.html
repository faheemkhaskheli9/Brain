Brain Ecosystem

    What is this?

    Insects sip nectar from flowers. Each season only one colour has nectar, and they must learn which. Foxes hunt insects and are chased off by humans. Humans pick berries, which grow in clumps.

    Every creature has its own brain that learns from reward alone: dopamine says "better than expected" or "worse", and only recently used synapses change. Insects are born with strong instincts and live short lives; humans are born nearly blank, live long, remember places and tell each other where food is.

    Click a creature to see its dopamine, its instincts and what it has learned. A newborn inherits its parent's instincts (slightly mutated) but none of what the parent learned.

    How it works

    One kind of brain, three sizes. Every creature runs the learner from Brain World: a fixed random sparse expansion of its senses feeds an actor-critic. Dopamine is the temporal-difference error, and a synapse changes only where its eligibility trace meets dopamine (a three-factor rule, no backprop). Insects have 60 hidden units, foxes 200, humans 400. These are game sizes, not neuron counts.

    Senses. Six 60° sectors around the head. In each it senses how close the nearest red flower, blue flower, ripe bush, insect, fox, human and wall is, plus one remembered place.

    Instincts and learned likes. Each species is born with a pull toward or away from each kind of thing (its genome): insects flee foxes and like flowers, foxes chase insects, humans barely care at first. On contact (nectar, an empty flower, a bite, being chased off) dopamine moves a learned like or dislike for that kind, as dopamine neurons do at the insect mushroom body. Learned likes fade slowly, so a soured food is tried again.

    Memory. A fox remembers, briefly, where it last saw prey. A human keeps a map of where berries were found (a stand-in for the hippocampus) and walks back there.

    Telling (a hypothesis-level model of culture): humans near each other update their maps toward what the other knows better. In our tests it did not make a newcomer eat more, perhaps because shared knowledge sends everyone to the same bushes. Toggle it and see.

    What is tested. node game/ecosystem/test_ecosystem.mjs checks that learning pays for every species and that grown foxes and humans beat newborns with the same genes. The numbers and their limits are in the README.
