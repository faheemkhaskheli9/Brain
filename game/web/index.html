Spark

A neuron adds up the charge that flows onto its membrane. When the voltage reaches threshold it fires a spike, drops below its resting level and is silent for a moment. Charge also leaks away, so input that arrives slowly never adds up. Each click (or Space) sends a 10 ms pulse of current into this model neuron. Time runs 20 times slower than real so you can watch it.

Fire exactly 5 spikes before the 500 ms window ends.

One click stays below threshold. Two quick clicks fire one spike, so send 5 pairs spread out.

You used temporal summation: pulses that arrive close together add up before the leak drains them away. Too many in a row and the neuron keeps firing. This is the leaky integrate-and-fire model, the workhorse of spiking simulations. Real neurons add ion-channel detail (Hodgkin-Huxley), but threshold, leak and reset are the core.
