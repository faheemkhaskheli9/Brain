# Key papers - annotated reading list

## Summary
One line per paper on what it found and where this knowledge base uses it,
grouped by theme. Full citations, DOIs and open-access PDF links for every
reference are in [research/papers.md](../research/papers.md) (generated by
`python research/fetch_papers.py`, which also saves open-access PDFs to the
git-ignored `research/pdfs/`). Rows tagged **hypothesis** are proposals or
frameworks, not settled findings.

## How it works

### Brain structure and scale
| Paper | Finding | Used in |
|---|---|---|
| Azevedo et al. 2009 | Isotropic fractionator count: ~86 billion neurons and a similar number of non-neuronal cells (not the folk 10:1 glia ratio); the cerebellum holds ~80% of neurons. | [[brain-structure-overview]] |
| Herculano-Houzel 2012 | The human brain is a scaled-up primate brain; it uses ~20% of resting energy. | [[brain-structure-overview]] |
| Scoville & Milner 1957 | Patient H.M.: bilateral medial temporal lobe removal caused lasting anterograde amnesia while skills and short-term memory survived - the hippocampus is needed to form new declarative memories. | [[brain-structure-overview]], [[memory-systems]] |
| Knowlton, Mangels & Squire 1996 | Parkinson's patients fail probabilistic habit learning but keep declarative memory; amnesics show the reverse - striatum vs medial temporal lobe dissociation. | [[brain-structure-overview]] |
| Bechara et al. 1995 | Amygdala damage abolishes conditioned fear responses but leaves knowledge of the pairing; hippocampal damage does the opposite. | [[brain-structure-overview]] |
| Feinstein et al. 2011 | Patient S.M., with bilateral amygdala lesions, does not experience fear. | [[brain-structure-overview]] |
| Funahashi, Bruce & Goldman-Rakic 1993 | Dorsolateral prefrontal lesions in monkeys impair memory-guided saccades - working memory needs prefrontal cortex. | [[brain-structure-overview]] |
| Barton 2008 | Occipitotemporal (fusiform) lesions cause acquired prosopagnosia (face blindness). | [[brain-structure-overview]] |
| Holmes 1917 | Cerebellar gunshot injuries cause ipsilateral ataxia, dysmetria and hypotonia - the cerebellum calibrates movement. | [[brain-structure-overview]] |
| Schmahmann 2003 | Thalamic strokes produce distinct syndromes by vascular territory, mirroring the thalamus's relay nuclei. | [[brain-structure-overview]] |

### Neurons
| Paper | Finding | Used in |
|---|---|---|
| Hodgkin & Huxley 1952 | Squid-axon action potential explained by voltage-gated Na+ and K+ conductances with gating variables; the template for every conductance-based model. | [[neurons-and-synapses]], [[levels-of-brain-modeling]] |
| Beniaguev, Segev & London 2021 | Reproducing a detailed cortical pyramidal neuron's input-output at millisecond resolution needs a deep temporal convolutional network; without NMDA receptors a shallow net suffices. | [[neurons-and-synapses]] |
| Izhikevich 2003 | A two-variable model reproduces most cortical firing patterns (regular, bursting, chattering) at near-LIF cost. | [[levels-of-brain-modeling]], [[spiking-neural-networks]] |

### Synaptic plasticity and reward
| Paper | Finding | Used in |
|---|---|---|
| Bliss & Collingridge 1993 | Review of hippocampal long-term potentiation (LTP) and its NMDA-receptor dependence as a synaptic memory mechanism. | [[learning-and-plasticity]] |
| Markram, Lübke, Frotscher & Sakmann 1997 | In neocortical pairs, the order of pre- and postsynaptic spikes within tens of ms decides potentiation vs depression - a founding spike-timing-dependent plasticity (STDP) result. | [[learning-and-plasticity]] |
| Bi & Poo 1998 | The canonical STDP curve: pre-before-post within ~20 ms potentiates, post-before-pre depresses. Reference data for Brain Lab's STDP scorecard. | [[learning-and-plasticity]] |
| Turrigiano 2008 | Synaptic scaling: neurons multiplicatively rescale all excitatory synapses to hold their firing rate - homeostasis that keeps Hebbian learning stable. | [[learning-and-plasticity]] |
| Schultz, Dayan & Montague 1997 | Dopamine neurons fire to unexpected reward, move to the predicting cue after learning, and dip when expected reward is omitted - the temporal-difference (TD) error. | [[learning-and-plasticity]], [[brain-inspired-learning-algorithms]] |
| Gerstner et al. 2018 | Eligibility traces let a later reward or neuromodulator signal credit synapses active seconds earlier (three-factor rules). | [[learning-and-plasticity]] |
| Kuśmierz, Isomura & Toyoizumi 2017 | Review of three-factor learning: Hebbian coincidence gated by a third, neuromodulatory signal. | [[learning-and-plasticity]] |
| Oja 1982 | A Hebbian rule with built-in weight normalisation converges to the first principal component of its input. | [[brain-inspired-learning-algorithms]] |

### Memory and continual learning
| Paper | Finding | Used in |
|---|---|---|
| McClelland, McNaughton & O'Reilly 1995 | Complementary learning systems: a fast hippocampus stores episodes and replays them to a slow, interleaved-learning neocortex, avoiding catastrophic interference. | [[memory-systems]] |
| Kumaran, Hassabis & McClelland 2016 | CLS updated: replay prioritisation, fast schema-consistent cortical learning, and the link to experience replay in deep RL. | [[memory-systems]], [[build-a-brain-like-learner]] |
| Buzsáki 2015 | Hippocampal sharp-wave ripples carry replay and are needed for consolidation and planning. | [[memory-systems]] |
| Hopfield 1982 | An energy-based recurrent network acts as content-addressable memory; capacity ~0.15·N patterns (later analysis: 0.138·N). | [[memory-systems]], [[neural-coding-and-computation]] |
| Ramsauer et al. 2020 | Modern continuous Hopfield networks have exponential capacity, and their update rule equals transformer attention. | [[memory-systems]] |
| Kirkpatrick et al. 2017 | Elastic weight consolidation (EWC): penalise changes to weights important for old tasks (Fisher information), inspired by synaptic consolidation. | [[memory-systems]], [[brain-inspired-learning-algorithms]] |
| Shin, Lee, Kim & Kim 2017 | Generative replay: a generator rehearses pseudo-samples of old tasks instead of storing data. | [[brain-inspired-learning-algorithms]] |

|---|---|---|

| O'Keefe & Dostrovsky 1971 | Hippocampal place cells fire when a rat is in one location. (2014 Nobel, with the Mosers.) | [[spatial-navigation]] |
| Taube, Muller & Ranck 1990 | Head-direction cells fire for one allocentric heading, independent of position. | [[spatial-navigation]] |
| Muller & Kubie 1987 | Changing the environment remaps place fields. | [[spatial-navigation]] |

| Fyhn et al. 2007 | Hippocampal remapping goes with shifts and rotations of the grid map. | [[spatial-navigation]] |
| Solstad et al. 2008 | Border cells fire along environment edges. | [[spatial-navigation]] |
| Fiete, Burak & Brookings 2008 | Several grid modules with different spacings form a residue-like code with very high positional capacity (theory). | [[spatial-navigation]] |
| Stensola et al. 2012 | Grid spacing comes in a few discrete modules with roughly constant ratios. | [[spatial-navigation]] |
| Müller & Wehner 1988 | Desert ants path-integrate: they return home along a straight vector. | [[spatial-navigation]] |
| Doeller, Barry & Burgess 2010; Jacobs et al. 2013 | Grid-like codes in humans: fMRI signal with six-fold symmetry, then single-neuron recordings. | [[spatial-navigation]] |
| Constantinescu, O'Reilly & Behrens 2016 | Humans use a grid-like code to navigate abstract 2-D concept spaces too. | [[spatial-navigation]] |
| Stachenfeld, Botvinick & Gershman 2017 | **Hypothesis**: place cells encode a successor representation (a predictive map of future states). | [[spatial-navigation]] |
| Banino et al. 2018 | A recurrent net trained to path-integrate develops grid-like units, and they help vector navigation in RL agents. | [[spatial-navigation]] |
| Whittington et al. 2020 | **Hypothesis**: the Tolman-Eichenbaum Machine - one model unifying spatial and relational memory, reproducing place and grid cells. | [[spatial-navigation]] |

### Credit assignment: brain vs deep learning
| Paper | Finding | Used in |
|---|---|---|
| Lillicrap, Cownden, Tweed & Akerman 2016 | Feedback alignment: fixed random feedback weights still train deep networks - exact weight symmetry is not required. | [[brain-vs-deep-learning]] |
| Lillicrap et al. 2020 | **Hypothesis**: the brain may approximate backprop by encoding errors as differences in neural activity (NGRAD). | [[brain-vs-deep-learning]] |
| Scellier & Bengio 2017 | Equilibrium propagation: a free and a nudged phase give a local contrastive update equal to the gradient in energy-based networks. | [[brain-vs-deep-learning]] |
| Hinton 2022 | Forward-Forward: two forward passes (real vs negative data) with a layer-local "goodness" objective replace the backward pass. | [[brain-vs-deep-learning]] |
| Sacramento, Costa, Bengio & Senn 2018 | **Hypothesis**: cortical microcircuits with apical dendrites can approximate backprop. | [[brain-vs-deep-learning]] |
| Payeur et al. 2021 | **Hypothesis**: burst-dependent plasticity lets one spike train multiplex feedforward signals and feedback errors. | [[brain-vs-deep-learning]] |
| Richards et al. 2019 | Framework: study the brain via objective functions, learning rules and architectures, as in deep learning. | [[brain-vs-deep-learning]] |
| Hassabis, Kumaran, Summerfield & Botvinick 2017 | Review of what AI took from neuroscience (attention, replay, episodic memory, continual learning) and what is left. | [[brain-vs-deep-learning]], [[build-a-brain-like-learner]] |
| Frank 2023 | Large language models train on orders of magnitude more language than children hear - the data-efficiency gap. | [[brain-vs-deep-learning]] |
| Zador 2019 | Much animal behaviour is innate, compressed through a "genomic bottleneck"; learning from scratch is not the brain's main strategy. | [[brain-vs-deep-learning]] |
| Zador et al. 2023 | NeuroAI white paper; proposes an "embodied Turing test". | [[build-a-brain-like-learner]] |
| Lake, Ullman, Tenenbaum & Gershman 2017 | Human-like learners need causal models, intuitive physics and psychology, compositionality and learning-to-learn. | [[build-a-brain-like-learner]] |
| Pathak, Agrawal, Efros & Darrell 2017 | Curiosity as forward-model prediction error in a learned feature space (intrinsic curiosity module). | [[brain-inspired-learning-algorithms]] |
| Schrimpf et al. 2018 | Brain-Score benchmarks neural networks against primate neural and behavioural data - the model for per-phenomenon brain-match scores. | [[build-a-brain-like-learner]] |

## Relevance to building brain-like AI
Start a new model from its row's paper: the finding is the target behaviour
and the figure is the data to score against (e.g. Bi & Poo for STDP, Schultz
for TD learning, Hopfield for capacity). Tag any borrowed hypothesis row as a
hypothesis in the code comments too.
